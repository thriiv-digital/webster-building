Local to Kiama, based in Shellharbour.

Kiama is best known for the Blowhole, but the town's history runs through dairy farming and basalt quarrying, and that older character still shows in the Federation and Californian bungalow-era homes scattered through the town centre and along Terralong Street. Alongside that, Kiama has grown steadily as a tree-change and holiday destination — plenty of owner-occupied family homes sit next to weekenders and short-stay rentals, and newer developments have filled out areas like Kiama Heights and Bonaira as the town has expanded north and inland toward Jamberoo.

That mix of older cottages, family homes and holiday properties means the trade work we do in Kiama is genuinely varied — and because we're a coastal town, salt-air exposure is a real, practical consideration in almost every job, whether that's choosing the right timber treatment for a deck or specifying a coastal-rated Colorbond fence. We've done real deck work in Kiama — the photos on this page and on our Decks & Pergolas page are from an actual local job, not a stock photo.

Whether you're renovating a character home near the town centre, maintaining a holiday let, or building something new further out toward Jamberoo, we bring the same trade-level precision and straightforward pricing to every job in Kiama — and there's no minimum job size, so a single repair is just as welcome as a full renovation.

Timelines vary with the job rather than the suburb, but as a general guide: a single repair or small fix-out is often turned around within a day or two once we're on site, while a full deck, fencing line or flooring job typically takes anywhere from a few days to two weeks depending on scale and any lead time on materials. For holiday properties, we're used to planning work around guest bookings rather than assuming the place is empty whenever suits us.

Fencing in Kiama

Fencing is where Kiama's coastal position matters most. Homes very close to the water may benefit from a coastal-rated Colorbond specification to handle salt-air exposure over the long term, and we'll assess your property and recommend the right option rather than defaulting to a standard product that might corrode faster than expected. Timber fencing remains a popular, more traditional choice too, particularly around the older parts of town, provided it gets the staining or oiling that coastal conditions make more necessary than they would be further inland.

Whichever material you choose, standard NSW height rules apply — generally 1.8m for side and rear boundaries, 1.2m facing the street — and we're happy to talk through what's right for your specific block and its exposure to wind and salt. For properties directly exposed to onshore winds, we'll also factor wind loading into post spacing and fixings, not just the material itself.

Fireplace installation in Kiama

Cooler, damper coastal winters make a properly installed fireplace genuinely useful in Kiama, not just decorative — and we see requests across the full range of properties, from older cottages near the town centre without an existing chimney, to newer coastal builds wanting a fireplace and feature wall specified from the start, to weekenders where owners want a cosy, low-fuss heat source for weekend visits.

As AHHA-certified installers, we work strictly to AS/NZS 2918 clearance and safety standards on every job, whether that's a full masonry retrofit, a zero-clearance wood heater built into a custom timber surround, or an electric fireplace with no flue requirements at all — a common choice for holiday homes where a real fire isn't practical between guest stays. Coastal humidity and salt-laden air can also affect flue fittings and flashing over time, so correctly specced, corrosion-resistant components matter more here than they would inland.

Flooring in Kiama

Kiama's mix of owner-occupied homes and short-stay rentals means flooring priorities differ depending on who's living there. For holiday lets and investment properties, hybrid flooring is a popular choice — it's water-resistant and stands up well to the wear and tear of frequent guest turnover, without the maintenance timber needs. For owner-occupied family homes, timber and engineered timber remain popular for their look and longevity, particularly in the character homes around the older parts of town.

Whichever you choose, subfloor preparation matters, especially in Kiama's older housing stock where decades of coastal humidity can affect the base a new floor goes down on. We check and prepare the subfloor properly on every job rather than laying straight over a problem that will show up within a year. Herringbone and other feature patterning is a popular upgrade for owner-occupied homes wanting a point of difference, while investment properties more often prioritise a straightforward, durable install over a decorative layout.
