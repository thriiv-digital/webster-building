Fireplaces

Costs vary depending on the type of fireplace, the complexity of the installation, and any custom surrounds or mantels involved. Get in touch for a free, no-obligation quote tailored to your space.
Yes. We hold Australian Home Heating Association (AHHA) certification. This guarantees your wood or electric fireplace installation complies strictly with Australian Standards (AS/NZS 2918) for safety, clearances, and structural integrity.
It depends on the type of fireplace and your home. Some installations fall under exempt development, while others may require council approval. We can help guide you through what's needed before work begins.
Placement depends on clearances to combustible materials, flue routing, and ventilation requirements. We'll assess your space and recommend the safest, most effective location for your fireplace.
Yes. Modern electric and zero-clearance wood fireplaces can be safely installed in homes without an existing brick chimney. We build custom timber framing, heat-shielded enclosures, and feature walls with appropriate flue systems to suit almost any interior room.
We'll walk you through how to safely operate and maintain your new fireplace. Keep an eye on wear and tear over time, and reach out if you ever notice damaged glass, firebricks, or fittings.
Most fireplace installations take one to a few days depending on the complexity, whether custom surrounds or feature walls are involved, and any lead time for materials.
Yes, think of a hearth as your floor's insurance policy. It's a non-combustible base (usually stone, concrete, tiles, or slate) that the fireplace sits on. Its job is to shield your beautiful timber floors or carpets from the extreme heat radiating downward, and to catch any rogue stray sparks or ash when you open the door to reload a log.
We treat your home like our own, using drop sheets and dust extraction to keep cleanup to an absolute minimum.
Surprisingly, bigger isn't always better! If you buy a massive heater for a small space, it will cook you out of the room. You'll end up turning the air vents down to stop sweating, which actually starves the fire of oxygen, makes the glass go completely black, and causes the flue to clog up with soot. We recommend looking at your room's square footage, ceiling height, and insulation to pick the perfect "sweet spot."
Stick to seasoned Australian hardwoods like Ironbark, Box, or Red Gum. "Seasoned" just means the wood has been split and left to dry out under a roof for at least a year. Avoid softwoods like pine (they spit sparks and burn way too fast) and never burn painted timber, old pallets, or treated fencing panels, as they release nasty chemicals.
Every heater model has tested safety clearances specified by the manufacturer to prevent surrounding walls and furniture from overheating. As AHHA-certified installers, we ensure all side, rear, and corner clearances strictly meet Australian Standard AS/NZS 2918. We'll let you know if your desired location is compliant.
Yes, we work with both Colorbond metal roofs and terracotta/concrete tiled roofs. We use specialised, weather-proof flashing kits (like DekTite or lead flanged flashings) and high-temp silicones to ensure the penetration through your roof is 100% watertight and storm-proof.
Ninety percent of the time, black glass is caused by burning wood that is still a bit green or damp. The fire spends all its energy boiling off the water in the wood rather than burning hot, creating dirty, sticky smoke that bakes onto the glass. Burning hot, dry hardwood and keeping your air vents open will usually burn that black soot right off!
Freestanding heaters sit out in the open on a hearth, radiating heat from all sides—making them great for heating larger, open-plan spaces efficiently. Inset or built-in fireplaces are recessed into a wall or custom stud frame, offering a sleek, flush look that's ideal for modern feature walls with integrated TVs and hidden cabling.
On the very first burn of the season, dust that gathered on top of the firebox and inside the flue over summer burns off, creating a faint smell. On a brand-new fireplace, the high-temperature metallic paint also needs one or two hot burns to fully cure, which produces a temporary chemical smell. Just open a window or door for the first hour and it will clear right up!
Yes! Traditional open brick fireplaces are notorious for sucking warm air out of the room up the chimney. We can install a modern, glass-fronted insert wood heater directly into your existing brick chimney opening, or re-frame the cavity to give you a massive boost in heating efficiency and a fresh look.

Absolutely! We offer no-obligation quotes for flooring installation projects. Contact us today to schedule a consultation and receive a personalised quote for your flooring needs.
We don't offer a fixed price per square metre. Every space is different, so we prefer to visit and assess your home in person. Pricing depends on the type of flooring you choose and whether any floor levelling or subfloor preparation is required.
Before flooring installation, please remove all furniture and items from the room that you can manage, ensure the subfloor is clean, and provide clear access to the installation area for our team. We'll handle the rest, including moving any heavy furniture, ensuring a smooth and efficient installation process.
A squeaky floor is usually caused by slight movement. If the subfloor underneath isn't perfectly flat, or if the flooring boards expand due to a sudden shift in weather, the boards can rub against each other or against the subfloor nails. When we install floors, we spend a lot of time prepping the subfloor to ensure it's dead flat and securely glued and nailed to prevent those annoying midnight squeaks.
Yes, but we can't just throw it right down. Concrete slabs hold moisture, which can ruin timber. We always apply a specialised moisture barrier over the concrete first. Depending on your home's layout, we will either install a plywood underlay first to nail the timber into, or use a high-bond acoustic glue system to adhere engineered boards straight to the slab.
Timber breathes. When the weather is humid, the wood absorbs moisture and swells; when the weather dries out (like in winter when you turn the heater on), the wood shrinks. A tiny bit of gapping during dry seasons is completely normal for solid timber. We leave hidden "expansion gaps" around the perimeter of the room underneath your skirting boards to give the floor room to move safely without buckling.
If we are installing pre-finished engineered floors, you can walk on them almost immediately! However, if we are sanding and coating a solid timber floor on-site, you'll need a bit of patience. Depending on the type of sealant we use (water-based vs. polyurethane), you'll usually need to stay off the floors entirely for 24 to 48 hours, and wait a few days before dragging heavy furniture back into the room.

Ninety percent of the time, deck failure isn't actually the fault of the boards—it's a lack of ventilation underneath. If a deck is built too low to the dirt without proper airflow, moisture gets trapped underneath. The bottom of the board expands while the sun dries out the top, causing the wood to cup, warp, and pull the screws right out. We always engineer proper drainage and airflow into our sub-frames to stop this from happening.
To keep a natural timber deck looking brand new, you should give it a good clean and a fresh coat of quality deck oil once a year (or every 6 months if it sits in direct, harsh coastal sunlight). If you let it go, it will naturally weather to a soft, silvery-grey color. If maintenance sounds like a chore, composite decking is definitely your best bet!

Fencing

Understanding local council regulations and building codes is essential when installing a fence. As a general rule, fences can be constructed without the need for any planning approval in the following cases: gates must not open outwards; on bushfire-prone land, fences must be constructed of non-combustible materials; side and rear boundary fences must not be higher than 1.8m; and fencing along the boundary of a primary or secondary road must not be higher than 1.2m. For more detailed requirements, we'd recommend checking the relevant NSW Government Planning fact sheet, or we're happy to talk it through with you.
Under NSW State Environmental Planning Policy (SEPP) exempt development rules, standard side and rear boundary fences up to 1.8 metres high usually do not require council approval, provided they meet specific material and location guidelines. We can guide you on what applies to your property.
Absolutely! Whether you're replacing a section of an existing fence or installing a new one, we can match the style and colour to seamlessly integrate with your current fencing.
If your neighbour is hesitant to upgrade a shared fence, it's essential to have open communication and discuss the benefits of upgrading together. However, if you and your neighbour can't agree about fencing work, the law says you have to follow a set process. Either you or your neighbour can serve a Fencing Notice. If you still can't reach an agreement after a month has passed, you or your neighbour can apply to the Local Court or the NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal (NCAT) for Fencing Orders. For more information on your rights, we'd recommend checking the Legal Aid website, or feel free to ask us.
Uneven terrain or obstacles such as trees or rocks can present challenges during fence installation. Our team is skilled in overcoming these obstacles and can provide solutions to ensure a seamless and aesthetically pleasing fence installation.
